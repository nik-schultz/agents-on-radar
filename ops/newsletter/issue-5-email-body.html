A scam-check bot for your parent, plus 3 AI jobs

This week's picks are about AI that looks out for you: a scam checker your mom or dad can text before sending money, an assistant that phones the car shops so you don't sit on hold, a morning brief for your meetings and a training check-in from your watch. Each prompt tells the AI to stop and ask you before anything is booked, sent or paid. Copy one, fill in the brackets, and try it.

Job 1 of 4

Share a scam-check bot your parent can text before they send money

Set up one bot with clear scam-checking instructions and share it with an older parent. When a scary call, text or email arrives ("your grandson is in jail"), they text the bot first and get a calm answer and one safe next step, like calling the relative back on a saved number. It's a second opinion, not a guarantee, so the prompt sends every money request back to a call with you.

You end up with: A patient 'second opinion' your parent can text any time, set up once by you, that pushes every money-under-pressure moment back to a phone call with family.

Prompt to copy

You are a scam checker for my [mom/dad], [name], who is [age] and not technical. When [name] sends you a message, a screenshot or describes a phone call, reply in 2-3 short, calm sentences: say whether it looks like a scam, name the warning signs, and give ONE safe next step. Treat any request for money, gift cards, wire transfers, payment apps, crypto, passwords or codes as a likely scam. For anyone claiming to be family in trouble, tell [name] to hang up and call that person back on the number saved in their contacts, or ask for our family safe word. Never ask [name] for passwords, codes, card or bank details. If money is about to move, tell [name] to call me first at [your number]. Do not contact anyone or click any links yourself.

Job 2 of 4

Get your car booked in for a service by letting an AI call the nearby shops

Tasks in Gemini became free on Nov 17, and in the US it can phone businesses for you (Grok Bot and paid Muse plans can too). The prompt has it call nearby shops, find a slot that fits your calendar and check with you before booking. It won't agree to repairs, prices or deposits on the call.

You end up with: A confirmed service appointment on your calendar, plus a short note of what each shop offered, without spending time on hold.

Prompt to copy

My [2019 Honda Odyssey] is due for [its regular service / a check-engine light check]. Please call the [two] nearest [dealerships / repair shops] near [my home / ZIP code] and find an appointment on [a weekday morning before 9am] in the next [4 weeks].

- Say you're an AI assistant calling for me.
- Only pick a time when my calendar is free, and skip shops that are walk-in only.
- Ask roughly how long it takes and whether they can give me a ride or a loaner.
- Don't agree to any repairs, prices or deposits, and don't give out my card or account details.
- Before you book, tell me what each shop offered and wait for my OK. Then add the appointment to my calendar with the address.

Job 3 of 4

Get a morning brief that pulls in the emails you need for each of today's meetings

Save this once as a daily task in Gemini, ChatGPT, Claude or Grok Bot. Each weekday morning you get one short page: what each meeting is about, the emails behind it, what you promised to bring and the replies you owe. It can draft replies, but it never sends, accepts or moves anything without your yes.

You end up with: One page to read over coffee that tells you what each of today's meetings needs from you, with the right emails already linked.

Prompt to copy

Every weekday at [7:00am], look at my calendar for today and make me one short page.

For each meeting:
- Time, who's in it, and what it's about in one line.
- The latest emails or messages with those people or on that topic (link them), and what was last agreed.
- Anything I promised to bring or send.

Then add:
- Replies I owe, with who and how many days late.
- Anything due this week from my reminders or emails with a date in them.
- Clashes: meetings that overlap or leave me less than [15] minutes to get between places.

Keep it under [250] words. Draft replies if useful, but never send, accept, decline or move anything until I say yes.

Job 4 of 4

Get a morning check-in from your fitness watch that says how you slept and what to train today

Connect your Garmin (or Oura, Strava or Withings) data to Claude, ChatGPT or Grok and get a short morning note on your sleep, HRV and recent training, with one suggested session for today. It shows you any workout before putting it on your watch. It's coaching guidance, not medical advice.

You end up with: A two-minute morning read on your recovery and training, plus an approved workout waiting on your watch if you want one.

Prompt to copy

You're my training check-in. Use only my [Garmin] data from the connector. If something is missing, say "no data" instead of guessing.

1. Last night: sleep time, HRV and resting heart rate, each compared with my own last [14] days.
2. This week so far: sessions done, total time, and how hard they were (time in each heart-rate zone).
3. Say in one line whether I look well recovered, normal, or tired, and which numbers made you say that.
4. Suggest one session for today that fits my goal of [a 10K on 15 March / getting fitter / losing 5 kg], plus an easier option if I feel worse than the numbers say.
5. If I ask you to put a workout on my watch, show me the exact session and date first and wait for my yes.

Keep it under 150 words. Don't change or delete anything in my account, and remind me this isn't medical advice if anything looks unusual.

One rule change to know

Citi, PNC and Citizens tell customers they're on the hook for anything their AI agents do in their bank accounts, even after a hack

Citi changed its online terms this week so that if you give a third-party AI agent your bank login and it makes a wrong payment, or the tool gets hacked, the losses are yours; JPMorgan and Bank of America announced similar policies last month. If your bot helps with money, keep it read-only (like summarizing statements) and make every payment yourself.
