Let AI book your flight and take errands from your car or watch

This week's picks are about handing jobs to AI when your hands are busy: booking a holiday flight, passing along errands from the car or your wrist, and letting agents search public data on a question you care about. Each prompt tells the AI to stop and ask you before anything is booked, paid or sent. Copy one, fill in the brackets, and try it.

Job 1 of 4

Book a flight with an AI agent (and check it before you pay)

Booking holiday travel? An agent can compare fares and fill in passenger details, then stop for your OK before paying. New this week: United now blocks personal AI agents from booking, so on some airlines you'll let the agent find the fare and finish checkout yourself.

You end up with: A booked flight with a confirmation code from the airline, after you approved the exact fare and passenger names.

Prompt to copy

Find me a flight from [city or airport] to [city or airport], leaving [date, time window] and returning [date, time window]. [Number] travelers, [cabin], [number] checked bags, at most [number] stop(s), budget [amount] total. Show me the 3 best options in a table: airline, flight numbers, times, total price including bags, and the change/refund rules. Book on the airline's own website unless I say otherwise. Passenger names exactly as on passports: [names]. Stop before payment and show me a summary of names, flights, total price and fare rules. Do not pay, accept paid extras (seats, insurance) or confirm anything until I reply "book it". If a site asks for a sign-in code or CAPTCHA, hand control back to me.

Job 2 of 4

Hand off tasks to your agent by voice while you drive

Since Nov 11, Gemini works on Apple CarPlay, so ChatGPT, Claude, Grok and Gemini can all be reached from the car screen. Talk through your to-dos on the drive, and the prompt makes it hold anything that sends or buys until you've parked and checked.

You end up with: A list of tasks started or finished during the drive, waiting for your review.

Prompt to copy

I'm driving, so keep replies short and spoken. I'll list tasks one at a time. For each one, repeat it back in one sentence and start it. Research and drafts are fine; don't send, post, buy or book anything until I review it later. When I hang up, write me a summary: done, in progress, waiting for my OK.

Job 3 of 4

Hand your agent a quick errand from your smartwatch

Meta's Muse agent now has an Apple Watch and Wear OS app. Ask for movie tickets or a calendar fix from your wrist and approve the result when it's ready. Save bigger payments for your phone, where you can read the details.

You end up with: Small errands started from your wrist and finished in the background, with you approving the final step.

Prompt to copy

[Say this to your agent from the watch] Find two tickets for [movie] near [area] this [day] after [time]. Check my calendar for clashes first. Show me the best two options and wait for me to approve before booking or paying.

Job 4 of 4

Let AI agents dig through public data on a hobby question (and try to prove you wrong)

A weekend project: two hobbyists pointed AI agents at free NASA data. One found what turned out to be an already-mapped Maya site; the other turned up two signals now listed as planet candidates. The prompt asks the agents to try to prove each lead wrong.

You end up with: A shortlist of real leads from public data, each with the evidence and the checks it passed, plus a draft note for the experts who can confirm it.

Prompt to copy

I'm curious whether [your question, e.g. there are unmapped old ruins in this region] using free public data from [source, e.g. NASA lidar / TESS]. First, write a plan: which data to download, what a real find would look like, and 5 tests that could prove a lead is NOT real. Then do the work in parallel batches and give me a morning report with your top 3 leads and the evidence for each. For every lead, search papers, catalogs and official registers to check whether it's already known, and tell me how sure you are. Don't contact anyone or submit anything without asking me.

Difficulty: Advanced · Fill in the [brackets] first.

One thing that went wrong

Hackers stole AI keys stored on app host Railway and ran up about $740,000 in charges

The attacker went looking for AI provider keys saved on Railway and used them to run other people's AI jobs on the victims' bills; Railway says it will pay what the AI companies won't refund. If you've ever put an OpenAI, Claude or Gemini key into a hosting or bot service, set a spending cap at the provider and swap the key if that service reports a breach.

Source: Railway blog, November 10, 2026
