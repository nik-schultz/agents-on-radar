Hand AI your shopping list, your raise talk and your day plan

This week's picks are small, everyday jobs: filling a grocery cart, rehearsing a pay conversation, planning your day and decoding a tax letter. Each prompt keeps you in charge, so the AI suggests and you decide. Copy one, fill in the brackets, and try it.

Job 1 of 4

Snap a photo of your handwritten shopping list and let an agent fill the cart

Snap your handwritten grocery list and Walmart's Sparky agent matches each item to your usual store and past buys. You check the cart and pay. It's for U.S. shoppers in the Walmart app and on walmart.com.

You end up with: A ready-to-check cart in a minute or two instead of typing every item into a search box, with out-of-stock items already flagged.

Prompt to copy

Here's a photo of my shopping list for [this week / the party on Saturday]. Match each item to my usual store. Where I've bought something before, use that; for anything new, show me [2] options and tell me why. Tell me what's out of stock and suggest a swap. If a line is hard to read, ask me instead of guessing. Don't place the order. I'll check the cart and check out myself.

Job 2 of 4

Prepare and practice negotiating your salary or a job offer

Review season is coming for a lot of people. Let AI help you pick a number from real pay data, draft a friendly counteroffer, then play your manager so you can rehearse first.

You end up with: A target number, a walk-away number, a short counteroffer email in your own words, and a few practice rounds against a realistic recruiter or manager.

Prompt to copy

Help me get ready to negotiate my pay.

Situation: [JOB OFFER / ASKING FOR A RAISE]
Job title and location: [TITLE, CITY OR REMOTE]
Offer or current pay: [BASE, BONUS, OTHER PERKS]
Real pay ranges I found: [PASTE RANGES AND WHERE THEY CAME FROM]
My strongest results: [2-3 THINGS I ACHIEVED, WITH NUMBERS IF I HAVE THEM]
Other things I'd value: [e.g. start date, remote days, extra vacation]

1. Suggest a target, a slightly higher ask and a walk-away number, and explain why in plain language. If my pay data is thin, tell me what to look up instead of guessing.
2. Draft a counteroffer or raise email under 150 words: thankful, confident, not demanding.
3. Then play the recruiter or my manager. Push back realistically ("that's above our budget", "this is our best offer"), one message at a time, and wait for my reply. After each reply, tell me one thing that worked and one phrase to improve.

Everything must stay true: never invent a competing offer, a job title or a number I didn't give you.

Job 3 of 4

Have your AI plan your day from your real calendar and to-do list

Say "plan my day" and an assistant connected to your calendar and task list proposes a time-blocked plan with breaks. Nothing goes on your calendar until you approve it.

You end up with: A realistic plan for today on your own calendar, built around your existing appointments, plus a short list of what moved to later.

Prompt to copy

Plan my day for [today, date]. Look at my calendar and my open tasks. My working hours are [9am-5pm]; I have the most energy in the [morning]. Rules: don't move any existing events; keep [lunch 12-1] free; add 10-minute breaks between blocks; if a task has no deadline or time estimate, ask me before scheduling it; if everything doesn't fit, tell me what to push to tomorrow and why. Show me the plan as a list first. Don't add anything to my calendar until I say yes.

Job 4 of 4

Understand your tax forms with AI, safely

Got a confusing tax letter or form? AI is good at explaining what a box means and listing questions for your preparer. Tests show it gets the math wrong, so don't let it fill in or file anything.

You end up with: A plain-English explanation of your forms, a list of documents to gather, and a short list of questions to check with IRS.gov, your tax software or a real tax professional.

Prompt to copy

Help me understand my taxes. Don't calculate my tax or tell me what to file; just explain and help me get organized.

Tax year and country/state: [e.g. 2026, US, Ohio]
My situation in general terms: [e.g. one job plus some freelance work, renting, one child]
Form or letter I'm confused about: [PASTE THE TEXT WITH SSN, ACCOUNT NUMBERS, NAMES AND ADDRESSES REMOVED]

1. Explain what this form or letter is and what each part means, in plain English.
2. Give me a checklist of documents I'll probably need to gather.
3. List the questions I should confirm on IRS.gov, in my tax software or with a tax professional.

If a rule changed recently or you aren't sure it's current, say so clearly instead of guessing.
