Have your bot catch calendar clashes hiding in your email

Plans don't only live in your calendar. A rescheduled event in a confirmation email, or a "call Mom at 2" note you emailed yourself, can quietly clash with what's booked. An always-on agent can compare your email with your calendar every morning and text you before a clash, but in WIRED's test of six agents only two caught one on their own, so ask for this check by name.

EasyUpdated Oct 30, 2026 · By BotsDoThat · Added Oct 30, 2026

Seen on X · Reddit · Official docs

What the bot does

  1. Every morning it reads the next few days of your calendar.
  2. It searches your email for anything with a date or time in it: confirmations, invitations, reschedule notices and reminders you sent yourself.
  3. It flags where the two disagree: a different date in the email than in the calendar, two things at the same time, or no travel time between two places.
  4. It texts or messages you with a short list and a suggested fix for each, well before the clash.
  5. It doesn't move, accept, decline or reply to anything until you say so.

What you end up with

A short morning heads-up, only when something clashes, so you don't show up on the wrong day or double-book yourself.

Copy-paste prompt

Fill in the [brackets]. Then paste it into any tool below.

Copy this prompt 
Every weekday at [7:30am], check my calendar for the next [3] days and search my email for anything that mentions a date or time: confirmations, invitations, reschedule notices and reminders I sent myself. Tell me only when something doesn't line up: a different date or time in an email than in my calendar, two things at the same time, or back-to-back events in different places with no travel time. For each one, give me a one-line fix. If nothing clashes, say so in one line or stay quiet. Don't move, accept, decline, delete or reply to anything without asking me first.

Setup

  1. Pick an agent that can read both your email and your calendar (for example ChatGPT Dots, Grok Bot, Meta Muse or Claude Duet) and connect Gmail or Outlook plus your calendar when it asks.
  2. Paste the prompt above and ask it to save it as a daily scheduled check, not a one-time answer.
  3. Test it once: put a fake event in your calendar, then email yourself a note about something else at the same time. See whether the bot flags it, the way WIRED tested six agents.
  4. If it misses the test, tell it exactly what it missed ("check emails I send myself, not just the calendar") and run the test again.
  5. Keep the rule that it only reports. You fix the calendar yourself, or tell it which change to make.

Watch out

  • Most agents didn't do this unprompted: in WIRED's test of six agents, only Dots and Claude Duet caught a clash between an emailed reminder and a calendar event (WIRED, Oct 27, 2026).
  • This needs broad access to your email and calendar. Only connect the accounts you're comfortable sharing, and keep work and personal accounts separate if your employer has rules.
  • Don't let it accept, decline or move meetings on its own. A wrong guess can cancel something important.
  • Recent emails can look more urgent than they are, and a messy calendar gives a messy report (r/AI_Agents).

What this costs to run

In a chat app it runs inside your plan. If you run it as a bot on an AI API, the bill depends on how often it runs and how much it reads.
