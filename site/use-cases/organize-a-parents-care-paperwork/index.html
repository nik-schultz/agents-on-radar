Organize an aging parent's care and paperwork

You're helping a parent with doctors, insurance, medicines and bills, and the details live in a dozen places.

MediumUpdated Oct 19, 2026 · By BotsDoThat · Added Oct 19, 2026Popularity: not measured

What the bot does

  1. Collect what you have: pill bottles, insurance cards, recent letters.
  2. Run the prompt without ID numbers.
  3. Fill in the gaps it lists.
  4. Keep the binder in a shared doc with siblings or caregivers.
  5. Log every call; it helps a lot if you ever need to appeal a decision.

What you end up with

One organized care binder: contacts, medicines, appointments, insurance details, and a running log of calls and decisions.

Copy-paste prompt

Fill in the [brackets]. Then paste it into any tool below.

Copy this prompt 
I'm helping my [MOM/DAD], age [AGE], with their care. Here's what I know so far (no ID numbers):
[DOCTORS, MEDICINES, INSURANCE TYPE, UPCOMING APPOINTMENTS, OPEN ISSUES]

1. Organize this into a care binder with sections: contacts, medicines (dose, time, prescriber), appointments, insurance, open issues.
2. List what's missing that I should collect.
3. Make a call-log template (date, who, what was said, next step).
4. Suggest questions to ask at the next appointment or care meeting.

Watch out

  • Double-check medicine names and doses against the labels. Errors here matter.
  • Get your parent's permission and any required forms (like a HIPAA authorization) before talking to their providers.

What this costs to run
