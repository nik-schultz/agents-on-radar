Get a morning brief that pulls in the emails you need for each of today's meetings

Set a daily task so your AI assistant checks your calendar each morning, finds the emails, messages and notes behind each meeting, and hands you one short page: what each meeting is about, what you owe people, and what clashes.

EasyUpdated Nov 18, 2026 · By BotsDoThat · Added Nov 18, 2026♥ 38 · ↻ 0 on X · measured Nov 19, 2026

Seen on X ×2 · Reddit · Official docs

What the bot does

  1. Runs on its own every weekday morning at the time you choose.
  2. Reads today's calendar and, for each meeting, finds the latest related emails or messages.
  3. Writes two or three lines per meeting: what it's about, what was last agreed and anything you promised to bring.
  4. Lists replies you owe and how many days late they are, plus anything due this week.
  5. Flags meetings that overlap or leave you no time to get there.
  6. Can draft replies for you, but doesn't send anything until you say yes.

What you end up with

One page to read over coffee that tells you what each of today's meetings needs from you, with the right emails already linked.

Copy-paste prompt

Fill in the [brackets]. Then paste it into any tool below.

Copy this prompt 
Every weekday at [7:00am], look at my calendar for today and make me one short page.

For each meeting:
- Time, who's in it, and what it's about in one line.
- The latest emails or messages with those people or on that topic (link them), and what was last agreed.
- Anything I promised to bring or send.

Then add:
- Replies I owe, with who and how many days late.
- Anything due this week from my reminders or emails with a date in them.
- Clashes: meetings that overlap or leave me less than [15] minutes to get between places.

Keep it under [250] words. Draft replies if useful, but never send, accept, decline or move anything until I say yes.

Setup

  1. Pick one assistant that can read both your email and your calendar (Gemini with Gmail and Google Calendar, Grok Bot on Mac with Mail and Calendar, or ChatGPT/Claude with those connectors switched on).
  2. Paste the prompt and fill in the brackets.
  3. Ask it to run this every weekday at the time you want. In Gemini, use Task mode and ask for a recurring schedule; in other apps use 'scheduled task' or 'routine'.
  4. Read the first brief and tell it what to cut or add (for example 'skip internal stand-ups' or 'include the Zoom link').
  5. Once a week, check the brief really arrived each day. A schedule that quietly stopped looks the same as a quiet morning.

Watch out

  • The brief is only as good as what it can see. If a meeting was arranged by phone or on another account, it will say little about it.
  • Keep 'never send without my OK' in the prompt. Drafting is safe; sending on your behalf is where mistakes cost you.
  • Your email and calendar go through the AI company. Check its privacy and history settings if your work email has sensitive material.

What this costs to run

In a chat app it runs inside your plan. If you run it as a bot on an AI API, the bill depends on how often it runs and how much it reads.
