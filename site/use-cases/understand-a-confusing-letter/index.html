Understand a confusing letter or notice, and let AI handle the next step

A letter from a government office, insurer, bank or landlord landed in your mailbox or inbox and you can't tell if it matters. Your AI explains it, checks it for scam signs, puts the deadline on your calendar and drafts the reply, asking you before each step.

EasyUpdated Nov 3, 2026 · By BotsDoThat · Added Oct 19, 2026

Seen on Official docs

What the bot does

  1. Reads the letter: a photo you upload, a PDF in your Google Drive, or the email it came in (with Claude's Gmail and Drive connectors turned on).
  2. Explains in plain English who sent it, what it wants, the deadline and what happens if you do nothing.
  3. Points out scam warning signs and tells you to check with the organization's official website, not the contact details in the letter.
  4. Asks you first, then adds the deadline to your Google Calendar with a one-line note of what to do.
  5. Drafts a short reply. If the reply goes by email, it sends it from your Gmail only after you approve it.

What you end up with

A plain-English summary, a scam check, the deadline on your calendar, and a reply ready to send once you've checked it.

Copy-paste prompt

Fill in the [brackets]. Then paste it into any tool below.

Copy this prompt 
I got a letter and I don't fully understand it.
[UPLOAD A PHOTO WITH PERSONAL NUMBERS COVERED, OR: It's the PDF called [FILE NAME] in my Google Drive / the email from [SENDER] in my Gmail.]

Please answer in plain English, at about an 8th-grade reading level:
1. Who is this from and what is it about, in 2 sentences?
2. Do I need to do anything? If yes, what exactly, and by what date?
3. What happens if I do nothing?
4. Are there signs this could be a scam (pressure, threats, odd payment methods, strange links)? Tell me how to check it using the organization's official website, not the contact details in the letter.
5. If there's a deadline, ask me first, then add it to my Google Calendar on that date with a one-line note of what I need to do, plus an event one week before.
6. If a reply is needed, write a short, polite reply with [BLANKS] for anything you don't know. Don't send anything until I say yes.
If anything is unclear or you are guessing, say so.

Setup

  1. In Claude, click + under the chat box, hover over Connectors and turn on Gmail, Google Calendar and Google Drive. Sign in with Google when it asks.
  2. If the letter came as an email attachment, save the PDF to Google Drive first. Claude's Gmail connector can see attachment names but not what's in them.
  3. Keep the default setting where Claude asks for your OK before each calendar event or email.
  4. For a paper letter, take a photo with Social Security, account and case numbers covered, and upload it.

Watch out

  • Scammers send official-looking letters too. The IRS warns that impersonators demand payment “now or else”, threaten arrest, and use odd links.
  • Remove Social Security numbers, account numbers, member IDs and birth dates before you paste or upload anything. In ChatGPT you can use Temporary Chat or turn off “Improve the model for everyone” under Settings › Data controls.
  • For anything with legal consequences (court papers, eviction, immigration, debt lawsuits), use the AI to understand it, then talk to a legal aid office or a lawyer.
  • Anthropic says it doesn't train on your Gmail, Drive or Calendar connector data. Text you copy from them into a chat follows your normal chat setting, so check that if you've allowed training.

Full guide

Most of us have a pile of letters we've been meaning to deal with. AI chatbots are excellent translators of official-speak. Quickly, you'll know whether a letter matters, what it wants, and how to reply, without guessing.

Step 1: Protect your personal information

Before anything goes into a chatbot, cover or delete: Social Security numbers, account and policy numbers, case numbers, dates of birth and signatures. The AI doesn't need them to explain the letter. If you use ChatGPT, Temporary Chat isn't used to train models (OpenAI says it may keep it up to 30 days for safety checks), and you can switch off “Improve the model for everyone” in Settings › Data controls.

Step 2: Ask for the four things that matter

  • Who sent it and what it's about.
  • Whether you need to act, and by when.
  • What happens if you don't.
  • Whether anything looks like a scam.

Step 3: Check it's real, using your own lookup

Don't call the phone number printed in a suspicious letter. Look up the organization's official website or the number on your card or account, and confirm there. The IRS's own scam page lists the warning signs: demands to pay “now or else”, threats of arrest or deportation, and odd or misspelled links. Ask the AI to point out any of those in your letter.

Step 4: Make the deadline impossible to miss

If Claude's Google Calendar connector is on, ask: “Add this deadline to my calendar with a one-line note, and add an event one week before.” By default Claude asks for your OK before it creates each event. With other chat apps, add it to your phone's calendar yourself. Many problems with letters come from missing a date, not from the letter itself.

Step 5: Reply

If the letter asks for a response, ask the AI for a short, polite reply that includes your reference number (add it yourself after), what you're sending, and a request for written confirmation. Read it and fix anything that isn't exactly true. If the reply goes by email, Claude (with Gmail connected) can send it from your Gmail once you approve it. If it goes by mail or a web portal, send it yourself.

When to get a human

Court papers, eviction notices, immigration letters and lawsuits about debt can have strict deadlines and serious consequences. Use the AI to understand what the paper says, then contact your local legal aid office or a lawyer the same week.

Questions people ask

Which AI tools can do this?

Claude with its Gmail, Google Calendar and Google Drive connectors does every step, including the calendar event and an email reply after you approve it. ChatGPT, Gemini and Grok can explain the letter and draft the reply; ChatGPT on paid plans can also send it from a connected Gmail or Outlook.

Can I just upload a photo of the letter?

Yes, most chat AIs read photos. Cover your account numbers, ID numbers and signature first.

Can the AI read the letter straight from my email?

Claude can search and read your Gmail once its Gmail connector is on, but it sees only the names of attachments, not what's inside them. Save a PDF letter to Google Drive and point Claude to it, or upload a photo.

Which AI is best for this?

Any big chat app (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Grok) can explain a letter well. To have it read the letter from your Drive, put the deadline on your calendar and send an email reply after you OK it, use Claude with its Google connectors turned on. The free plan works.

How do I know the AI got it right?

Ask it to quote the exact sentence each answer comes from. Then check those sentences in the letter yourself.

How hard is it to set up?

It's rated Easy. Start with the steps under “What the bot does”.

What should I watch out for?

Scammers send official-looking letters too. The IRS warns that impersonators demand payment “now or else”, threaten arrest, and use odd links.
