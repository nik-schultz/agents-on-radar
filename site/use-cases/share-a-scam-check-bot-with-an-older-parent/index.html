Share a scam-check bot your parent can text before they send money

Set up one AI bot with clear scam-checking instructions and share it with an older parent. When a scary call, text or email arrives ('your grandson is in jail'), they text the bot first and get a calm, plain answer and the safe next step, like calling the relative back on a saved number.

EasyUpdated Nov 13, 2026 · By BotsDoThat · Added Nov 13, 2026♥ 161 · ↻ 11 on X · measured Nov 13, 2026

Seen on X · Official docs

What the bot does

  1. Waits for your parent to text it, forward a message or describe a call in their own words.
  2. Points out the warning signs it sees, such as pressure to act fast, a secret ('don't tell Mom'), or a request for gift cards, a wire, a payment app or crypto.
  3. Tells them the safe next step in one or two short sentences: hang up, call the relative back on the number saved in their contacts, or ask for the family safe word.
  4. Never asks for passwords, codes or bank details, and tells them to call you before any money moves.
  5. Remembers the family details you gave it (names, the safe word reminder, your number), so they don't have to explain each time.

What you end up with

A patient 'second opinion' your parent can text any time, set up once by you, that pushes every money-under-pressure moment back to a phone call with family.

Copy-paste prompt

Fill in the [brackets]. Then paste it into any tool below.

Copy this prompt 
You are a scam checker for my [mom/dad], [name], who is [age] and not technical. When [name] sends you a message, a screenshot or describes a phone call, reply in 2-3 short, calm sentences: say whether it looks like a scam, name the warning signs, and give ONE safe next step. Treat any request for money, gift cards, wire transfers, payment apps, crypto, passwords or codes as a likely scam. For anyone claiming to be family in trouble, tell [name] to hang up and call that person back on the number saved in their contacts, or ask for our family safe word. Never ask [name] for passwords, codes, card or bank details. If money is about to move, tell [name] to call me first at [your number]. Do not contact anyone or click any links yourself.

Setup

  1. Pick a family safe word together first (the FCC suggests one that only family knows), and agree that nobody sends money after a surprise call without calling back.
  2. Create the bot: in Grok Bot, click + > Create a Bot, name it something friendly (e.g. 'Scam Check'), and paste the prompt above as what it should help with. In ChatGPT or Claude, paste it into the custom instructions or a project on your parent's own account instead.
  3. Grok Bot: when it's ready, click Publish, then Share > People you invite, and add your parent as a Collaborator so they can chat but not change it. Sharing needs a Team or Enterprise plan; check yours.
  4. Put the bot on your parent's home screen (or pin the chat) and practise once together with a fake 'grandson in jail' text.
  5. Tell them the one rule: if anyone asks for money or a code in a hurry, text the bot and call you first.

Watch out

  • A bot can miss a new scam or flag a real message. Treat it as a second opinion; the safe step is always to hang up and call back on a saved number.
  • Scammers now clone voices with AI and spoof caller ID (FCC). 'It sounded just like him' is not proof.
  • In a shared Grok Bot, each person's chat is private, so you won't see what your parent asked. That's why the prompt tells them to call you before money moves.
  • Don't give the bot your parent's bank logins, card numbers or email access. It only needs to read what they paste in.
  • If money was already sent, call the bank or payment app right away and report it to the FTC (reportfraud.ftc.gov) and local police.

In a chat app it runs inside your plan. If you run it as a bot on an AI API, the bill depends on how often it runs and how much it reads.
