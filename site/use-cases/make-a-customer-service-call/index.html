Have your bot make a customer-service phone call for you

Some jobs still need a phone call, like cancelling a service or sorting out an account. With a phone-calling add-on, your bot can make the call, sit on hold and report back.

MediumAdded Oct 20, 2026♥ 63 · ↻ 3 on X · measured Oct 21, 2026

What the bot does

  1. Gets a phone number of its own through a calling add-on you connect.
  2. Looks up the company's customer-service line and the account details it will need.
  3. Calls, says up front that it's an AI assistant calling for you, and asks for exactly what you said (in the shared example, cancelling a streaming service).
  4. Sticks to your rules: it turns down offers and agrees to nothing you didn't approve.
  5. Tells you what was said and saves any confirmation number.

What you end up with

The call handled (for example, a service cancelled), a plain summary of what was said and any confirmation number, without you sitting on hold.

Copy-paste prompt

Fill in the [brackets]. Then paste it into any tool below.

Copy this prompt 
Call [company] at their customer service line and cancel my [subscription name]. My account details: [name on account, email, last 4 of card if needed]. Start the call by saying you're an AI assistant calling on my behalf. Turn down any offers unless it's [what you'd accept], and don't agree to anything else. Afterwards, tell me what they said and give me the confirmation number.

Watch out

  • Some companies won't act for anyone but the account holder. One user says an airline refused to rebook his flight for exactly that reason.
  • Have the bot announce it's an AI. One user says a restaurant owner almost hung up on his bot, thinking it was spam, until it identified itself.
  • A phone call is harder to undo than an email, so be specific about what the bot may agree to.
  • Check afterwards that the change really happened (for example, the next charge doesn't appear).
