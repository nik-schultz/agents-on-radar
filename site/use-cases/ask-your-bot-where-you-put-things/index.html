Tell your bot where you put things, then just ask it later

Every time you store, lend or move something, tell your bot in one line. Months later, ask 'where are the spare batteries?' or 'what's in bin 4?' and it answers with the room, shelf or box.

EasyUpdated Nov 19, 2026 · By BotsDoThat · Added Nov 19, 2026

Seen on Reddit · Official docs

What the bot does

  1. Keeps one running list of your things and where each one lives (room, shelf, box or bin).
  2. Updates the list when you text or say a move, like 'camping stove to garage, bin 4' or 'ladder lent to Sam'.
  3. Answers 'where is...' and 'what's in...' questions from that list.
  4. Can read a photo of a box's contents and add each item.
  5. Can remind you about things tied to an item, like returning something you borrowed or changing a filter.

What you end up with

A searchable list of what you own and where it is, kept up to date by one-line messages instead of a spreadsheet.

Copy-paste prompt

Fill in the [brackets]. Then paste it into any tool below.

Copy this prompt 
Be my 'where is it' memory. Keep one list of my things with: item, where it is (room > shelf/box), who has it if lent, and the date of the last change.

When I message a move like '[item] to [place]' or '[item] lent to [name]', update the list and reply with one line confirming it.
When I ask 'where is [item]?' or 'what's in [box]?', answer from the list only. If it isn't on the list, say so; don't guess.
If I send a photo of a box, list what you see and ask me before adding it.
Every [month], send me anything marked 'lent' for more than [30] days.
Never delete an item without asking me.
Counts toward your daily streak. Saved in this browser only.

Setup

  1. Pick where the list lives: a home-inventory app that connects to your chat app (Wheredai and OnHand both work with ChatGPT and Claude), or a simple note or spreadsheet your bot can edit.
  2. Name your places once: rooms, then shelves or numbered bins. Write the bin number on the bin.
  3. Paste the prompt and start with one area, like the garage or the hall closet. Snap a photo of each box and let it list the contents.
  4. From then on, send a one-line message whenever you put something away, move it or lend it.
  5. Test it: ask for three things you know the location of. If it gets one wrong, correct it straight away.

Watch out

  • It only knows what you tell it. If someone else moves things and doesn't say, the answer will be out of date. Share the list with your household.
  • A list of what you own and where can be sensitive. Keep valuables and safes out of it, or in a private section only you can see.
  • These apps use saved records, not tracking. It can't find something you never logged.
