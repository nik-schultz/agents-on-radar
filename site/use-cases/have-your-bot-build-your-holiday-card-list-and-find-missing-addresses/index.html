Have your bot build your holiday card list and find missing addresses

Addresses for holiday cards end up scattered across last year's spreadsheet, old emails and text messages. A personal agent can gather them into one clean list, flag the ones that look wrong or missing, and hand you a file your card service or label printer can use. You check the list and decide who gets a card.

EasyUpdated Nov 26, 2026 · By BotsDoThat · Added Nov 26, 2026♥ 6 · ↻ 0 on X · measured Nov 26, 2026

Seen on X

What the bot does

  1. Pulls names and addresses from the places you point it to: last year's list, your contacts, and (if you allow it) emails and texts where people sent their address.
  2. Merges everything into one sheet with name, street, city, state, ZIP and a status column, and removes exact duplicates.
  3. Flags rows with a missing ZIP or apartment number, an address older than a year, or two different addresses for the same person, without guessing a fix.
  4. Drafts a short 'what's your current address?' note for anyone it can't find, for you to send yourself.
  5. Exports the list in the format your card service or label sheet needs (for example a CSV for an upload, or a label PDF).

What you end up with

One checked address list for this year's cards, a short to-check list, a file ready to upload or print, and a sheet you can reuse next year.

Copy-paste prompt

Fill in the [brackets]. Then paste it into any tool below.

Copy this prompt 
Help me build this year's holiday card list.

1. Start from [last year's list: a Google Sheet / Excel file / note] and my contacts. If I say yes, also search my email and texts for messages where someone sent their address. Read only.
2. Put everyone in one sheet with: name as it should appear on the envelope ([e.g. The Garcia Family]), street, apartment, city, state, ZIP, where you found it, and a status column.
3. Remove exact duplicates. Flag anyone with a missing ZIP or apartment number, an address older than a year, or two different addresses. Never make up or 'correct' an address; flag it for me.
4. For anyone you can't find, draft a short friendly 'what's your address these days?' message. Don't send it; I'll send it myself.
5. When I've checked the list, export it as [a CSV for my card service / Avery 5160 label PDF].

Rules: keep the addresses private, don't share the sheet, and don't email, text or order anything.

Setup

  1. Decide where the finished list will live (a Google Sheet, Excel or Notion) and what the end file must look like. If you use a card service, download its address template first, so the bot can match the columns.
  2. Connect only what the job needs: your sheet and contacts first. Turn on email or message search only if many addresses live there, and keep it read-only.
  3. Paste the prompt with your details, and let it build the first version.
  4. Spot-check a handful of addresses and read every flagged row yourself. Send the 'what's your address?' messages from your own phone.
  5. Export the file, upload or print it, and keep the sheet. Next year, ask the bot to start from it and add a 'sent us a card' column.

Watch out

  • Text-message and email search reads a lot of private conversations. Turn it on only for this job, set it to read-only and ask-before-each-search, and switch it off afterwards (Khe Hy, Nov 19).
  • Bots can misread a messy address. Never let it 'fix' one silently; have it flag rows and check those yourself before anything prints.
  • The list is other people's personal data. Don't paste it into shared links or public chats, and don't let the bot contact anyone for you.
  • Mail is slow in December: one card service says to send by December 11 for Christmas delivery in the US (Picpost).

What this costs to run

In a chat app it runs inside your plan. If you run it as a bot on an AI API, the bill depends on how often it runs and how much it reads.
