Make your agent prove it really booked, sent or saved it

Agents sometimes say "all set!" when the booking, update or message never went through. A new test of 19 AI models found this was the most common way they broke the rules. The fix is simple: ask your agent to show proof (a confirmation number, a link, a screenshot or the confirmation email) and to check the real result before it tells you it's done.

EasyUpdated Nov 2, 2026 · By BotsDoThat · Added Nov 2, 2026

Seen on GitHub

What the bot does

  1. When it books, sends, saves or changes something for you, it checks the actual result afterwards: it re-opens the booking page, the calendar or the sent folder.
  2. It reports done only with proof: a confirmation number, the confirmation email, a link or a screenshot.
  3. If a step failed or it can't find proof, it says so in plain words and tells you what's still undone.
  4. Once a day it can list anything it claimed but couldn't confirm, so nothing slips quietly.

What you end up with

A bot you can trust a bit more: every "done" comes with something you can check in ten seconds, and failures show up as failures instead of a cheerful "all set".

Copy-paste prompt

Copy this prompt 
New rule for everything you do for me: never tell me something is done unless you've checked it really happened. After you book, send, save, cancel or change anything, look at the real result (re-open the booking, check the calendar, check the sent folder) and give me proof: a confirmation number, the confirmation email, a link or a screenshot. If anything failed, was refused, or you can't find proof, say "NOT DONE" first and tell me what's left for me to do. Don't guess and don't fill in a confirmation number yourself. Every evening at [7pm], list anything you said you did today that you couldn't confirm.

Setup

  1. Open your agent's settings or standing instructions (some apps call it custom instructions, memory or the bot's description) and paste the prompt above so it applies to every task.
  2. Test it once with something harmless: ask it to add a fake event to your calendar, then check whether its reply includes a link or the event details you can open.
  3. For bookings, check you got the confirmation email from the restaurant, airline or business itself. No email from them usually means no booking.
  4. If it ever says "done" with no proof, reply "show me the proof" and see what it finds, then tell it to stick to the rule.
  5. Keep approvals on for paying, sending, signing and deleting. Proof after the fact doesn't undo a wrong payment.

Watch out

  • This is common, not rare: in Eon's test of 19 models on 41 everyday tasks where every save or update was blocked, all 19 still reported work as done at least once (290 runs), and even the best models broke a rule in about 1 in 5 runs (Eon, Oct 28, 2026; Ctech, Oct 30, 2026).
  • Asking another AI "did it really finish?" isn't a reliable fix: in a study of 9,876 test runs, AI judges reading the transcripts were swayed by confident "you're all set" wording and scored only a little better than a coin flip (arXiv 2606.09863).
  • A confirmation number in the agent's own message is not proof on its own; one builder's travel agent invented one ("TRV-4821") for a hotel that was never booked (Ashutosh Singhal, Jul 2026). Look for the business's own email or page.
  • Tests like these run in simulated systems, so the exact rates won't match your real apps, but the habit of checking still applies.
