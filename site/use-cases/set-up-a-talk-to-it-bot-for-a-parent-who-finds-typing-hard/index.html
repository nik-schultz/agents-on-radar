Set up a talk-to-it bot for a parent who finds typing hard

If your mom or dad struggles with small keyboards (arthritis, a tremor, poor eyesight), set them up with an assistant they can just talk to. While you're home for the holidays, put it on their phone, turn on voice, and practise their real to-do list together: a letter to understand, an item to sell, a form to fill in. They stay in charge of anything that sends, pays or signs.

Seen on X · Official docs

What the bot does

  1. Listens and answers out loud, so your parent can ask for help without typing.
  2. Explains letters, bills and forms they photograph, in short, plain sentences with numbered steps.
  3. Works through their backlog one job at a time: drafting a reply, writing a listing for furniture they don't need, putting appointments in the calendar.
  4. Reminds them of the house rules: no passwords or card numbers in the chat, and check health, money and legal answers with a person.
  5. Asks before anything gets sent, posted, bought or signed.

What you end up with

A patient helper on their own phone that they can use by voice on any ordinary Tuesday, set up in about 15 minutes during one visit, with clear rules they agreed to.

Copy-paste prompt

Fill in the [brackets]. Then paste it into any tool below.

Copy this prompt 
Hi! I'm [parent's first name]. Typing is hard for me because of [arthritis / my eyesight], so I'll mostly talk to you.

Please always answer in plain, simple English, in short sentences, and number any steps. Ask me one question at a time.

Help me with my list, one job at a time: [e.g. explain the letter I'll show you, write a listing for the armchair I want to sell, put my eye appointment on my calendar].

Rules: never ask me for passwords, bank or card numbers or my Social Security number. Never send, post, buy, pay or sign anything until I say yes out loud. If something is about my health, money or a legal matter, remind me to check with [my doctor / my son or daughter / a professional].

Setup

  1. Pick one app that fits their phone: Muse (voice mode, US), ChatGPT, or Gemini on Android. One app is plenty.
  2. Install the official app (check the maker is Meta, OpenAI or Google) and sign them in with their own account, not yours, so their chats stay private.
  3. Make the text bigger, put the app next to Phone and Messages on the home screen, and show them the voice button.
  4. Let them hold the phone. Paste the prompt above (with their name and their real list) and practise two or three real jobs together.
  5. Write the three rules on a card by the phone: no passwords or card numbers, check health and money answers with a person, and 'if it says act now, call family first'. Call a week later and ask what they used it for.

Watch out

  • Scammers target older adults; the FBI reports people 60+ lost nearly $7.7 billion to fraud in 2025 (Future Leaders in AI). Agree on a family code word for urgent calls in the same visit.
  • AI can be confidently wrong. Health, money and legal answers are a starting point to check with a doctor, a professional or family (Ask It Simply, Future Leaders in AI).
  • Don't let the bot pay, sign or move money on their behalf. Keep 'ask me first' for every send, post and purchase.
  • Use their own account, not yours, and close any upgrade offers. The free version is enough to start (Ask It Simply).
