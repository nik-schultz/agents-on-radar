Put together a home or car insurance claim with an AI agent

Drop your photos, receipts and policy into one folder and let an agent build the damage list, a dated timeline and the questions to ask your insurer. You check every line and file the claim yourself.

EasyUpdated Oct 28, 2026 · By BotsDoThat · Added Oct 28, 2026

Seen on Official docs

What the bot does

  1. Put everything in one folder: photos and videos of the damage, receipts, repair quotes, the police report if there is one, and copies of your policy documents.
  2. Point an agent that can work on a folder, like Claude Cowork, at it and paste the prompt. It reads the photos and receipts and builds a room-by-room (or part-by-part) list of what was damaged, with the file each item comes from.
  3. It writes a short, dated timeline of what happened and a list of what's still missing, like a receipt, the claim number or the adjuster's name.
  4. It pulls your deductible, any deadlines and the documents your policy asks for out of the policy, quoting the page, and drafts the questions to ask your insurer.
  5. You report the claim yourself in your insurer's app, website or phone line. After every call, add who you spoke to, when and what they said to the agent's contact log.

What you end up with

A claim folder with a damage list that points to your photos and receipts, a dated timeline, your deadlines and the questions to ask, ready for you to submit and for the adjuster's visit.

Copy this prompt 
Work only in the folder [claim folder]. It has photos, receipts and my [home/car] insurance policy for [what happened and when, e.g. a burst pipe in the kitchen on March 3]. 1) Make a spreadsheet of every damaged item: item, room or part, what's wrong, which photo shows it, and the receipt or proof file. Leave the value blank unless a receipt shows it. 2) Write a dated timeline from the photo dates and my notes. 3) From the policy, list my deductible, any deadline for reporting the claim or sending documents, and the documents it asks for, quoting the page. 4) List what's missing and the questions I should ask my insurer. Don't guess values, don't add anything I didn't tell you was lost, don't contact anyone, and don't delete or move any files.

Setup

  1. Home: go room by room taking photos and video of the damage and list everything damaged. The NAIC says you'll need that list when you meet the adjuster. If you have no records, photos on your phone or from family and friends can help rebuild it.
  2. Car: photograph the cars, license plates, damage and street signs, plus the other driver's insurance card and license, and note witnesses, the time, place and weather (Texas Department of Insurance).
  3. Many insurers let you report a claim and upload photos in their app; check which way yours prefers (Insurance Information Institute).

Watch out

  • Report the claim right away; don't wait for the folder to be perfect. The NAIC says how long you have to report a claim varies by state.
  • Only claim what you really lost, at real values. AI can misread a photo or a receipt, and you're the one who signs the claim, so check every line. Adding items or inflating values is insurance fraud.
  • The AI's reading of your policy can be wrong. Confirm the deductible, coverage and deadlines with your insurer or agent; the III suggests asking about time limits for filing, for disputes and for sending more information.
  • Don't let the agent tidy up the folder. Keep every original photo and receipt, and work on copies (see how to keep an agent from deleting your files).
  • Your policy, photos and receipts are personal. Connect only the claim folder, not your whole drive, or use a local tool like Hob.

Questions people ask

Can AI file an insurance claim for me?

It can do the paperwork, not the filing. An agent can turn your photos and receipts into a damage list, pull deadlines from your policy and draft questions, but you should report the claim, talk to the adjuster and sign the forms yourself, because you're responsible for what's in them.

What do I need to file a home insurance claim?

The NAIC lists your policy number, name, address and phone number, an explanation of what happened and how bad the damage is, a list of the damaged property, and photos and videos of the damage. Find out your deductible first.

Is it safe to give an AI my insurance documents?

Share only the claim folder, not your whole drive, and use an agent that asks before it contacts anyone or deletes files. If you'd rather nothing is uploaded, a local app like Hob reads the files on your own computer.
