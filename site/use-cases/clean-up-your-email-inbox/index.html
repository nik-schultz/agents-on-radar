Get your email inbox under control

Thousands of unread emails, and the important ones get buried under newsletters and promos.

EasyUpdated Oct 19, 2026 · By BotsDoThat · Added Oct 19, 2026

Seen on Reddit · YouTube

What the bot does

  1. Export or copy your top senders (most email apps can sort by sender).
  2. Run the prompt and set up the suggested filters.
  3. Unsubscribe from the senders on the list.
  4. Use the AI for drafts; read every reply before sending.

What you end up with

A simple folder or label system, a list of senders to unsubscribe from, and short replies drafted for the emails that matter.

Copy-paste prompt

Fill in the [brackets]. Then paste it into any tool below.

Copy this prompt 
Help me get my inbox under control. Here's a list of my most frequent senders and some recent subject lines (no personal details):
[PASTE LIST]

1. Group senders into: must read, nice to read, unsubscribe, and archive.
2. Suggest 4–6 labels or folders and simple rules/filters to sort new mail automatically, with steps for [GMAIL / OUTLOOK / APPLE MAIL].
3. For these emails I need to answer: [PASTE], draft short, friendly replies.

Watch out

  • If you connect an AI to your email account, check what it can read and do, and require it to ask before sending.
  • Don't paste emails that contain passwords, codes or financial details.

Questions people ask

Can an AI read and sort my email for me?

Some assistants can connect to Gmail or Outlook and draft or organize messages. Check what your plan allows, and keep sending in your own hands.

Which AI tools can do this?

ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Grok, Gemini Spark or Grok Bot (personal agents) can do this today. Paste the copy-paste prompt on this page into any of them and fill in the [brackets].

How hard is it to set up?

It's rated Easy. Start with the steps under “What the bot does”.

How much time can it save?

In a 6-month field experiment with 7,137 knowledge workers at 66 firms, workers who used a generative-AI tool built into their email and other work apps spent two fewer hours on email each week in the second half of the experiment. Source: Dillon, Jaffe, Immorlica and Stanton, “Shifting Work Patterns with Generative AI”, American Economic Review: Insights.
