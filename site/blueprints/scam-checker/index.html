Scam Checker

Get a plain verdict on suspicious emails and texts before anyone in the family clicks.

EasyRough setup time: about 10 minutes
Goal
Get a plain verdict on suspicious emails and texts before anyone in the family clicks.
Runs
⏱ Every morning, plus any time you forward something suspicious
Watches
👁 New email (read-only), or messages you forward to it

What it does, step by step

  1. Reads new emails, or anything you forward.
  2. Looks for scam signs: fake sender, lookalike link, urgency, payment request.
  3. Gives a risk level and the exact red flags.
  4. Tells you the safe next step.

Paste these starter instructions

Copy, paste into your tool, and replace the [brackets] with your details.

Starter instructions 
You are my Scam Checker.
For each message you check, answer in this format:
VERDICT: Confirmed scam / High suspicion / Possible / Looks fine
RED FLAGS: (sender address, links that don't match, urgency, requests for money, gift cards, passwords or codes)
WHAT TO DO: one or two safe steps (for example: don't click, delete, report it, or call the company on a number from their official website).
Rules: you are read-only. Never click links, open attachments, reply or delete.

What this costs to run

If you run this bot yourself on an AI API: our assumption is 30 runs a month, each reading about 3,000 tokens and writing about 300 (one morning sweep of flagged messages plus the odd forwarded one, about 3,000 tokens a day). That's about 90,000 tokens in and 9,000 out a month.

How to set it up

  1. Pick one boring job. Choose ONE small job you do every week, like reading school emails or checking a web page.
  2. Pick your tool. Use what you already have.
  3. Paste the instructions and fill in the [brackets]. Open a new chat in the tool.
  4. Say “Run this once now and show me the result”. Don't schedule anything yet.
  5. Then say “Do this every [day/time]” and lock it down. Once the result looks right, ask it to repeat on a schedule.

Safety notes

  • Read-only, always.
  • A 'looks fine' verdict isn't a guarantee. When money is involved, call the company on a number you already trust.
