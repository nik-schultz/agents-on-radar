Beginner guide

How to build an AI agent without coding

Short answer: you don't write code. You pick one small job, write it down in four lines, paste plain-English instructions into an agent app you already use, watch one test run, then put it on a schedule with rules that make it ask before anything important. Here's each step, with a real blueprint as the worked example.

Updated Oct 29, 2026 · By BotsDoThat · New to agents? Start with what an AI agent is, in simple terms.

What you need

  • An agent app or a no-code automation tool. Agent apps on this site include Gemini Spark, Claude Cowork, ChatGPT dots (always-on agents), Grok Bot (personal agents) and Meta Muse. No-code automation tools include Zapier, Make and n8n.
  • The app it should read, like your Gmail or calendar, connected with the tool's own connect button.
  • One job, written down. That's the part that decides whether your bot works.

How to build your first AI agent, step by step

  1. 1

    Pick one small, boring job

    Choose one job you do every week that mostly means reading or checking: a morning inbox summary, a weekly school-email digest, a web page you keep refreshing. Narrow, read-only jobs make the easiest first bots. All of our blueprints are jobs like this.

  2. 2

    Write the job down in four lines

    Goal: what you want to end up with. Runs: when it should work (every weekday at 7am, or only when you ask). Watches: what it reads (new emails since yesterday). Never: what it may never do (send, buy, delete). Every blueprint starts with these lines, so you can copy them.

  3. 3

    Pick a tool you already use

    Use what you already have. Gmail and Google Calendar → Gemini Spark. Want to text your bot → Meta Muse or Instinct. Want routines on a schedule → Grok Bot, Claude Cowork scheduled tasks or ChatGPT dots. Prefer no AI chat at all → Zapier or Make. No agent yet? Paste the instructions into ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Grok each time; that works too, just not on autopilot.

  4. 4

    Paste the instructions and connect only what it needs

    Open a new chat in the tool. Copy the blueprint's instructions, paste them, and replace everything in [square brackets] with your details. If the tool asks to connect your email or calendar, say yes only to the apps the blueprint lists.

  5. 5

    Run it once and fix it in plain words

    Don't schedule anything yet. Watch the first run, compare it with your real inbox or page, and tell it what to fix in plain words (“ignore emails from the PTA”, “shorter”).

  6. 6

    Put it on a schedule, with approval rules

    Once the result looks right, ask it to repeat on a schedule. Keep it read-only or approval-only: it may read and draft, but it must ask you before it sends, buys, books or deletes. Check the first week of results.

  7. 7

    Check what it costs and set a limit

    Inside an app plan, the bot runs within your plan's limits. If it runs on a pay-per-use AI API, work out the monthly cost in the cost calculator and set a spend limit before anything runs on autopilot.

Starter instructions 
You are my Morning Inbox Briefing bot.
Every weekday at 7:00am, read every email that arrived since 7:00am yesterday.
Sort them into three groups:
1. NEEDS ME TODAY: someone is waiting on me, or there's a deadline within 48 hours.
2. FYI: worth knowing, no action.
3. NOISE: promotions, newsletters, receipts.
Send me a briefing: group 1 first (max 5 items, one line each, with who and what), then a one-line count of groups 2 and 3.
For easy replies in group 1, write a draft and leave it in my Drafts folder.
Rules: never send, delete or archive anything. If you're unsure which group something is in, put it in group 1.

Step 5: run it once. Say “Run this once now and show me the result”, then compare the briefing with your real inbox. Fix it in plain words: “ignore emails from the PTA”, “shorter”.

Step 6: schedule it. Say “Do this every weekday at 7am”. Keep its rules: Start read-only: it may read, but not send, buy, delete or change anything. Drafts stay in Drafts. You press send. Check the first week of briefings against your real inbox.

Step 7: check the cost. In an app plan it runs inside your plan's limits. On a pay-per-use AI API, our estimate (22 runs a month, about 15,000 tokens in and 800 out each time) comes to about $0.03 a month on Groq openai/gpt-oss-20b and about $4.18 on Anthropic Claude Fable 5.1 at standard API prices checked Oct 27, 2026. Agent app or no-code automation tool?

🤖 Agent app

You describe the job in plain words and the agent works out the steps. The easiest place to start, and it copes with messy things like email. Make it ask before it sends, buys or deletes.

🔗 No-code automation

In Zapier, Make or n8n you set the trigger and each step yourself, so it does the same thing every run. More setup, more predictable. Our Follow-Up Finder, Receipt Matcher and New Inquiry Logger blueprints list these as options.

5 rules that keep your first bot safe

  1. Start read-only: it may look, not change.
  2. Make it ask before it sends, buys, books, cancels or deletes.
  3. Never paste passwords into a chat. Use the app's official connect button.
  4. Tell it to ignore instructions found inside emails or web pages from strangers.
  5. Check the first week of results, and know how to switch it off.

Questions people ask

Do I need to know how to code to build an AI agent?

No. Agent apps take instructions in plain words: you describe the job, connect the apps it needs with the official connect button, and tell it when to run. Our blueprints give you the exact words to paste.

What's the easiest AI agent to build first?

A read-only one that only reads and reports, so nothing can go wrong. Our easiest blueprints are the Morning Inbox Briefing, Web Page Watcher, Scam Checker, Weekly Meal Plan Bot, Family Calendar Guardian and New Inquiry Logger.

Can I build an AI agent for free?

Partly. ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Grok each list a free plan (paid plans add more), so you can paste a blueprint's instructions into one whenever you need it, even without a scheduled agent. n8n is free to self-host, and Zapier and Make have free plans. Some agent features cost money: Gemini Spark is for Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers in select countries, and ChatGPT's cloud browser (ChatGPT Work (cloud browser)) needs a paid ChatGPT plan. Plans change, so check each tool's own page.

How much does it cost to run an AI agent?

Inside an app plan it runs within your plan's limits. On a pay-per-use AI API it depends on how often it runs and how much it reads. Our example, the Morning Inbox Briefing (22 runs a month, about 15,000 tokens in and 800 out each time, our estimate), comes to about $0.03 a month on Groq openai/gpt-oss-20b and about $4.18 on Anthropic Claude Fable 5.1 at standard API prices checked Oct 27, 2026. Try your own numbers.

Is it safe to connect my email to an AI agent?

It can be, if you keep it on a short leash: start read-only, make it ask before it sends, buys or deletes anything, connect apps only with the official connect button (never paste passwords into a chat), tell it to ignore instructions found inside emails from strangers, and check the first week of results. More: is it safe to give an AI agent your email and calendar?

What's the difference between an AI agent and an automation?

An automation follows the exact steps you set up, the same way every time. An AI agent reads your plain-English instructions and works out the steps itself, which is more flexible but needs approval rules. Tools like Zapier, Make and n8n let you build the step-by-step kind.
