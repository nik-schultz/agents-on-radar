Your settings to change

Every setting we checked, for all five assistants. Each has the in-app path and a link to the vendor page we checked it on. Where a vendor doesn't document the exact steps, we say so instead of guessing.

Grok Bot by SpaceXSI (xAI)

Grok Bot uses connected apps and a shared cloud computer. Auto Review checks each action, and your own Ask first rules always stop a matching action for your OK.

  1. Add Ask first rules for the actions you care about

    Ask first rules always stop a matching action and show you an approval card. If an Ask first rule and an Allow rule both match, Ask first wins.

    Where: Settings → General → Bot → Auto-review (keep it on) → Custom Rules → under “It should:” choose Ask first → Add Rule

    Source: Grok Bot docs: Approvals, security, and privacy · checked Nov 19, 2026

  2. Set each connected app's send, post and delete tools to Needs approval or Blocked

    Since v1.2.3 (Nov 18, 2026) each tool on a connector page can be Always allow, Auto-review, Needs approval or Blocked. Keep read-only tools you trust as they are.

    Where: Connect apps (sidebar) → select the connected count → open the app → set each tool

    The four per-tool choices come from the changelog; the sidebar steps come from the Settings docs. Needs Grok Bot 0.70.2 or later.

    Source: Grok Bot changelog (v1.2.3, Nov 18, 2026), Grok Bot docs: Settings and notifications · checked Nov 19, 2026

  3. Remove a saved card if you want to approve every payment

    A card saved in Vault fills in at checkout without a separate approval each time.

    Where: Settings → Vault → select the card → remove it

    Source: Grok Bot docs: Approvals, security, and privacy · checked Nov 19, 2026

  4. Stop Bots running commands on your own computer

    Access to the computer in front of you is separate from the Bot's cloud computer. The default is Ask every time; the docs suggest Never allow unless a Bot needs your local files.

    Where: Settings → Computer → Your computer → Execution on this computer → Never allow

    Source: Grok Bot docs: Approvals, security, and privacy · checked Nov 19, 2026

  5. Sign out of sites your Bots no longer need

    All your Bots share one cloud computer, so a site signed in for one Bot is available to the others. The docs say separate Bots are not a security boundary.

    Exact steps not documented. There's no single settings switch for this in the docs. Ask your Bot to sign out of the site, or revoke the app in that service.

    Source: Grok Bot docs: Approvals, security, and privacy · checked Nov 19, 2026

ChatGPT dots by OpenAI

A dot works on its own cloud computer with the apps you connect. An automatic review checks actions against your instructions, permissions and custom rules; some steps, like changing a password, always stay with you.

  1. Add custom rules for sending, paying and deleting

    Each rule picks one behavior: Take action without asking, Take action when you say so, Ask before taking action, or Hand off to you. Asking for a draft does not authorize sending it.

    Where: Desktop and web: Settings → Personalization → Custom rules (under Permissions) → Add. Phone: sidebar → Customize → Custom rules

    Source: ChatGPT Learn: Control your dot · checked Nov 19, 2026

  2. Review plugin permissions for each connected app

    Plugin permissions control app actions separately from custom rules. Rules don't grant or remove app access.

    Where: Custom rules → Open Plugins → Plugin permissions

    Source: ChatGPT Learn: Control your dot, OpenAI Help: Dots privacy, security, and safety FAQs · checked Nov 19, 2026

  3. Make purchases a Hand off to you rule if you want to finish every checkout yourself

    With Wallet, OpenAI says you approve each payment with biometrics. A Hand off to you rule makes the dot ask you to take the action instead.

    Where: Settings → Personalization → Custom rules → Add → describe “buying or paying for anything” → Hand off to you

    Source: ChatGPT Learn: Control your dot · checked Nov 19, 2026

  4. Check what your dot does in the background

    Pausing a dot doesn't stop every delegated task or cancel scheduled runs. Each has its own stop control.

    Where: Desktop app: your dot's profile → Activity (stop a task) and Scheduled (disable or delete a recurring task)

    Source: ChatGPT Learn: Control your dot · checked Nov 19, 2026

  5. Only connect your own computer if a task needs it

    OpenAI says your computer stays separate from the dot unless you choose to connect it.

    Exact steps not documented. We couldn't find documented steps for disconnecting a personal computer from a dot. Workspace admins have an “Allow local computer access” switch.

    Source: OpenAI: Introducing dots (updated Nov 3, 2026) · checked Nov 19, 2026

Claude Duet by Anthropic

Claude Duet runs on your Claude account, with the same memory and connectors. Anthropic says it is designed to ask before it sends a message, pays, signs a document or deletes anything.

  1. Set connector tools to Needs approval or Blocked in Claude

    Duet uses the connectors in your Claude account. Each connector has Tool permissions: Always allow, Needs approval or Blocked, per group of tools or per tool.

    Where: Claude → Customize → Connectors → select the connector → Tool permissions

    The Tool permissions steps are from Claude's general connectors docs. The Duet article doesn't say whether Duet follows each per-tool choice.

    Source: Claude docs: Get started with connectors, Claude Help: Get started with Claude Duet · checked Nov 19, 2026

  2. Disconnect apps Duet doesn't need

    Disconnecting stops access from then on, but Duet keeps memory of what it saw in earlier conversations.

    Where: Claude → Customize → Connectors → select the connector → Disconnect

    Source: Claude docs: Get started with connectors, Claude Help: Get started with Claude Duet · checked Nov 19, 2026

  3. Turn off past-chat search if you don't want Duet reading old chats

    Duet searches your Duet chats and your Claude chats outside projects, using Claude's Search and reference chats setting.

    Where: Settings → Your Claude → Search and reference chats (older layout: Settings → Memory)

    Source: Claude Help: Get started with Claude Duet · checked Nov 19, 2026

  4. Know how Duet pays before you connect Link or Shop Pay

    Anthropic says Duet asks before each purchase and uses a one-time card for each one. Link and Shop Pay are connected inside Duet, not in the connectors directory.

    Exact steps not documented. Anthropic's article doesn't list the steps to disconnect Link or Shop Pay from Duet.

    Source: Claude Help: Get started with Claude Duet · checked Nov 19, 2026

Meta Muse by Meta

Muse uses Connectors and the web. You set default permissions for connectors and websites, and each approval card offers choices from Allow once to Always allow.

  1. Set Connectors to Always ask

    Ask for some actions asks before every write action and important reads. Always ask asks before any action.

    Where: Settings → Permissions → Connectors → Always ask

    Source: Meta Help: How Muse works with your guidance and approval · checked Nov 19, 2026

  2. Set Web access defaults to Always ask

    Always ask makes Muse ask before it opens any website. Ask for some actions only asks when your information may be shared or the site is unfamiliar.

    Where: Settings → Permissions → Web access defaults → Always ask

    Source: Meta Help: How Muse works with your guidance and approval · checked Nov 19, 2026

  3. Revoke websites you allowed once and forgot

    Choosing “Allow for this site” on an approval card lets Muse act on that site later without asking again. This list shows those sites and lets you revoke them.

    Where: Settings → Permissions → Manage permissions → Allowed websites

    Source: Meta Help: How Muse works with your guidance and approval · checked Nov 19, 2026

  4. Make connectors read-only where Meta offers it

    Meta says many Connectors can be set up so Muse only retrieves data and can't take actions, like sending a calendar invite.

    Where: Settings → Permissions → Manage permissions → Connectors

    Source: Meta Help: How Muse works with your guidance and approval, Meta Help: How Muse works with Connectors · checked Nov 19, 2026

  5. Decide whether Muse reads new email on its own

    Connecting email may let Muse read new emails as they arrive. Meta says you can change this in Settings.

    Exact steps not documented. Meta's help page says “in Settings” but doesn't name the exact screen.

    Source: Meta Help: How Muse works with Connectors · checked Nov 19, 2026

Gemini Tasks by Google

Tasks in Gemini can use Connected Apps, Google Pay and Wallet, and a local or remote browser. Google says it always asks before sending, deleting, buying, paying or submitting forms.

  1. Disconnect apps your tasks don't need

    Tasks can use Connected Apps, including Gmail, Calendar, Drive, Google Pay and Wallet, and third-party apps.

    Where: gemini.google.com → sidebar → Customize (or More → Customize) → Connectors → the app → More options → Disconnect

    Source: Gemini Apps Help: Use & manage Connected Apps in Gemini, Gemini Apps Help: Manage your tasks & workflows in Gemini Apps · checked Nov 19, 2026

  2. Pause schedules that touch anything sensitive

    Google says if a schedule runs while you're offline, you may not be able to stop an unintended action.

    Where: Open the task thread → scheduled task chip → More options → Pause (or use the Scheduled page on the web)

    Source: Gemini Apps Help: Manage your tasks & workflows in Gemini Apps · checked Nov 19, 2026

  3. Delete the remote browser's saved sign-ins

    When Gemini uses its remote browser, cookies with your sign-in details are kept for later sessions until you delete them or they expire.

    Where: gemini.google.com → Settings → Browser → Delete remote browser data → Delete

    Source: Gemini Apps Help: Manage your tasks & workflows in Gemini Apps · checked Nov 19, 2026

  4. Turn off Chrome auto browse on computers you share

    With your local Chrome, Gemini can reach every site you're signed in to.

    Where: Chrome → More → Settings → AI Innovations → Gemini in Chrome

    Source: Gemini Apps Help: Manage your tasks & workflows in Gemini Apps · checked Nov 19, 2026

  5. Check bulk changes to Google Tasks reminders yourself

    Google says Gemini can make bulk changes to your private Google Tasks without asking first.

    Exact steps not documented. There's no setting for this in Google's page. Review the request before you send it, or manage the reminders in Google Tasks yourself.

    Source: Gemini Apps Help: Manage your tasks & workflows in Gemini Apps · checked Nov 19, 2026

Built from your Step 3 answers. The rules are the same everywhere; the tabs say where each assistant takes them.

Questions

What is Bot Checkup?

A free, 3-minute guide to the settings that control what Grok Bot, ChatGPT dots, Claude Duet, Meta Muse or Gemini Tasks can do with your accounts. You pick what's connected; we show the vendor-documented settings to change and one set of house rules to paste in.

Does it connect to my accounts?

No. It runs in your browser. Nothing you pick is sent to us. It can't see what your assistant has done.

Is this a security score or audit?

No. There are no scores or “safe” badges. It is a guide to settings each vendor documents, with links to their help pages. Use the vendor's own activity log to check what happened.

What is Checkup Watch?

A planned paid add-on that would save your setup and email you when a vendor change affects it, with the setting to change. It isn't built yet. You can join a free interest list; there is no checkout and no charge today.

What's free vs paid?

The checkup, house rules and parent mode are free forever. Checkup Watch ($5/month for one person, $9 for a household of up to 5) is a proposed paid plan that isn't live. Joining the interest list only adds you to our free weekly email.
