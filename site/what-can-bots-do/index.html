What is an AI agent? (in simple terms)

Short answer: an AI agent is an AI assistant that does a job for you, not just answers questions. Agents are good at reading, watching, sorting and drafting. They're getting OK at booking and buying while you watch. They aren't safe to trust with your money, your address or decisions you can't undo.

Updated Oct 24, 2026 · By BotsDoThat

What is an AI agent?

An AI agent is an AI assistant that can take steps on its own to finish a job you give it. You describe the goal in plain words (“every weekday at 7am, read my new email and tell me what needs a reply”), connect the apps it needs, and it does the reading, clicking and typing for you. Many agents can repeat the job on a schedule, and good ones stop and ask before anything important.

On this site we call them bots. Everyday examples: a bot that catches deadlines the moment they land in your inbox, one that watches a web page and pings you when it changes, and one that makes a customer-service call for you. See all 94 AI agent examples.

AI agent vs. chatbot: what's the difference?

💬 Chatbot

You ask, it answers. It can write a letter, explain a confusing form or make a plan, but you do the clicking. ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Grok all work like this for free.

🤖 Agent (a “bot”)

You give it a job, and it does the steps: opens websites, reads your email, fills in forms, and can repeat the job on a schedule. Examples: Meta Muse, Gemini Spark, Grok Bot, ChatGPT Work and dots, Claude Cowork, Instinct, plus no-code tools like Zapier, n8n and Make.

Most agents run on their own computer in the cloud, so they keep working when your laptop is shut. Google says Gemini Spark “keeps working in the background even when you close your laptop” (Google).

✅ What AI agents are good at today

  • Reading and summarizing: inbox briefings, turn the flood of school emails into one weekly digest, explaining a confusing letter.
  • Watching for changes: have a bot watch any web page and ping you when it changes, get alerted the moment your airline changes your flight, watch your email for one important letter or reply.
  • Drafting: complaints, follow-ups, replies in your style.
  • Doing the same small job every week: plan a week of family meals and fill the grocery cart, catch family calendar clashes before the week starts.
  • Spotting problems: get a daily scam check on your inbox, only see the emails that actually need a reply.

👀 OK if you stay in the loop

These work for many people, as long as the bot asks you before it pays or confirms:

  • book restaurant tables by message, even at phone-only places, book a haircut or appointment, even when the form won't work and plan and book a whole trip by texting your bot.
  • let an agent reorder your regular groceries: some stores block agents, so check which ones work.
  • have your bot make a customer-service phone call for you: tell it exactly what “resolved” means.

OpenAI says its agent asks before bookings and payments (OpenAI help); Google says Spark asks before “high-stakes actions like spending money or sending emails” (Google).

🛑 What they can't do safely yet

  • Get it right every time. A benchmark of four personal agents found that even when they found the right information, they “often miss what matters, overshare, or make things up” (micro1, Oct 2026).
  • Follow your exact conditions. WIRED's reviewer said her agent cancelled a delayed food order and she lost $64, even though she'd told it to cancel only with a refund (WIRED).
  • Keep your private details private. One reported case: an agent selling an item gave a buyer an address and booked a pickup when the seller wasn't home (X post). WIRED also notes reports of an agent keeping inbox copies after being disconnected.
  • Be faster than you. In one startup's timed test, agents took about 4½ to 14+ minutes to book a dinner the founder booked in 37 seconds (X post).
  • Get in everywhere. Some sites block agents. One user says Amazon blocked his (X post).
  • Replace a professional. Use bots to organize and draft. Medical, legal and money decisions stay with you and a qualified person. Be wary of anyone promising AI miracles; the U.S. FTC has acted against deceptive AI claims (FTC Operation AI Comply).

7 rules that keep you safe

  1. Start read-only. Let it look before it does anything.
  2. Make it ask before it sends, buys, books, cancels or deletes.
  3. Pay with a low-limit or single-use virtual card (give your agent a single-use card before it buys anything).
  4. Never give it your passwords in a chat. Use the app's official “connect” button.
  5. Tell it to ignore instructions found inside emails or web pages from strangers.
  6. Check the first week of results against the real thing.
  7. Keep a way to switch it off, and know which apps it's connected to.

More everyday jobs to try

  • Paperwork and letters: understand a confusing letter or notice, and what to do next, get through a long form without the headache, review a rental lease before you sign, write a complaint that actually gets a problem fixed, have a bot sort your scanned paper mail and flag what needs you, organize an aging parent's care and paperwork.
  • Writing in your voice: teach a bot to write emails that sound like you, write a letter of recommendation for someone, write a resignation letter that keeps the door open, write listings that sell your stuff faster.
  • Plans and bookings: have an ai agent find and book an appointment for you, plan a trip itinerary that fits your pace, make a moving checklist and change your address everywhere, get 5 weekend activity ideas that fit your kids and the weather.
  • Learning and side projects: use ai for homework help that teaches instead of cheats, get a short morning digest on topics you care about, test a side hustle idea before you commit.

Questions people ask

What is an AI agent, in simple terms?

An AI agent is an AI assistant that does a job for you instead of only answering questions. You give it a goal, like “read my new email every morning and tell me what needs a reply”, and it takes the steps itself: opening websites, reading your email, filling in forms, and repeating the job on a schedule.

What's the difference between an AI agent and a chatbot?

A chatbot answers when you ask, and you do the clicking. An agent does the steps for you, can keep working on a schedule, and should ask before it sends, buys, books or deletes anything.

Is an AI agent the same as a bot?

On this site, yes: we say “bot” for an AI agent that runs a job for you. Older “bots” followed fixed scripts; today's AI agents read plain-English instructions and decide the steps themselves.

What can AI agents do for me?

Today they're good at reading and summarizing your email, watching web pages and flight times for changes, drafting messages and forms, and running the same small job every week. With you checking, they can book tables, haircuts and trips, fill grocery carts and make customer-service calls.

Are AI agents safe to use?

Start with read-only jobs, make the agent ask before it sends, buys or deletes anything, connect apps only with the official connect button, and check the first week of results. Don't trust them yet with your money, your address or decisions you can't undo.
