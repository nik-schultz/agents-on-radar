3 things that changed

  1. 1
    QwAlibaba🧠 model releaseYou use this

    Alibaba fully releases Qwen4-Max, its top model, at about a quarter of the price of the big US flagships

    What it means for you: The preview from last week is now a regular paid model: $1.50 per million tokens in and $4.50 per million out on QwenCloud, 25% cheaper than Qwen3.8-Max, with a 1-million-token memory, image and video reading and tool use. It's mostly for people building their own bots who want a cheaper engine for coding and office tasks; Alibaba's claim of ~60% lower cost per task comes from its own test, so your savings may differ.

    Source: Alibaba Cloud · Also: QwenCloud pricing, eWeek · 14.9K likes on X

  2. 2
    OAOpenAI🏢 industryYou use this

    OpenAI starts a science blog with a physicist explaining how GPT-6 Astra helped him attack a 50-year-old physics problem

    What it means for you: It's a sign of how far the top models have come: a UC Santa Barbara physicist says GPT-6 Astra took the lead on the detailed proof behind a candidate way to define the Standard Model on a grid, with human experts checking it. He calls the result a candidate that still needs expert review, so it's a research milestone, not a new feature in ChatGPT.

    Source: OpenAI · 3.8K likes on X

  3. 3
    TcTencent🧠 model releaseYou use this

    Tencent releases Hy4 flash, a very cheap model aimed at everyday office tasks

    What it means for you: Hy4 flash costs about $0.07 per million tokens in and $0.28 out, a small fraction of most big-name models, and handles documents, research, slide decks and simple computer use with a 1-million-token memory. It's mainly for people who build their own bots and want a cheap engine for routine jobs; Tencent's claim that it matches Claude Haiku 5.5 comes from its own blind tests, and it's free in Tencent's WorkBuddy app through Nov 30.

    Source: Tencent · Also: PR Newswire, Dataconomy · 2.8K likes on X

🧰 Job of the day New on the site

Share a scam-check bot your parent can text before they send money

Set up one AI bot with clear scam-checking instructions and share it with an older parent. When a scary call, text or email arrives ('your grandson is in jail'), they text the bot first and get a calm, plain answer and the safe next step, like calling the relative back on a saved number.

🏡 Family & homeEasyWorks in: Grok BotChatGPTClaude

You end up with: A patient 'second opinion' your parent can text any time, set up once by you, that pushes every money-under-pressure moment back to a phone call with family.

Copy today's prompt 
You are a scam checker for my [mom/dad], [name], who is [age] and not technical. When [name] sends you a message, a screenshot or describes a phone call, reply in 2-3 short, calm sentences: say whether it looks like a scam, name the warning signs, and give ONE safe next step. Treat any request for money, gift cards, wire transfers, payment apps, crypto, passwords or codes as a likely scam. For anyone claiming to be family in trouble, tell [name] to hang up and call that person back on the number saved in their contacts, or ask for our family safe word. Never ask [name] for passwords, codes, card or bank details. If money is about to move, tell [name] to call me first at [your number]. Do not contact anyone or click any links yourself.

Watch out: A bot can miss a new scam or flag a real message. Treat it as a second opinion; the safe step is always to hang up and call back on a saved number.
