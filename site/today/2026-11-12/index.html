3 things that changed

  1. 1
    GGoogle🧠 model releaseYou use this

    Google releases Gemini 4 Hydrogen, a fast Gemini 4 model for everyone, including free users

    What it means for you: Your Gemini app, Google Search and Gemini in Sheets now get a quicker Gemini 4 model that Google says follows instructions more closely and picks the right tools more often, so everyday tasks like email drafts, comparisons and Gmail or Drive lookups need less back-and-forth. Free Gemini users get it through auto model selection; developers pay $1.50 per million input tokens and $7.50 per million output tokens, with a free tier in the Gemini API.

    Source: Google blog · Also: Gemini API pricing · 7.5K likes on X

  2. 2
    OAOpenAI✨ featureYou use this

    ChatGPT's dots now follow your own instructions, like 'show me a draft before sending any emails'

    What it means for you: You can now tell your ChatGPT dot how to behave and which actions need your go-ahead, for example always asking before it sends an email, and it keeps following that. OpenAI also says dots reply faster with less jargon; set it in the chat or under Customize, then Behavior.

    Source: ChatGPT What's new · Also: Gadgets 360 · 4.1K likes on X

    ⚠ Touches ChatGPT dots: check your settings →
  3. 3
    AnAnthropic✨ featureYou use this

    Claude can now join your Google Meet calls to take notes and build docs and whiteboards while you talk

    What it means for you: If your workplace pays for Claude Team or Enterprise, you can add Claude to a Google Meet call and it writes the notes and action items, answers questions in the chat, and drafts docs or whiteboards that everyone on the call can edit. It's a beta for Google Meet only for now, notes and answers are free during the beta, and you should check that everyone on the call is fine with being transcribed.

    Source: Claude blog · Also: Claude Help Center: Use Claude Meetings, VentureBeat · 81.4K likes on X

🧰 Job of the day New on the site

Let AI agents dig through public data on a hobby question (and try to prove you wrong)

Two non-scientists just used teams of AI agents on free public data: one found a Maya ruin hidden under jungle in NASA lidar scans overnight, the other turned up two planet candidates that NASA's TESS team has now listed. You pick the question and the rules; the agents do the hours of looking, checking and writing up.

💼 Work & careerAdvancedWorks in: ClaudeChatGPTGemini

You end up with: A shortlist of real leads from public data, each with the evidence and the checks it passed, plus a draft note for the experts who can confirm it.

Copy today's prompt 
I'm curious whether [your question, e.g. there are unmapped old ruins in this region] using free public data from [source, e.g. NASA lidar / TESS]. First, write a plan: which data to download, what a real find would look like, and 5 tests that could prove a lead is NOT real. Then do the work in parallel batches and give me a morning report with your top 3 leads and the evidence for each. For every lead, search papers, catalogs and official registers to check whether it's already known, and tell me how sure you are. Don't contact anyone or submit anything without asking me.

Watch out: Agents can miss earlier work. The Maya site turned out to be already mapped in a 2025 paper; the agents missed it during their literature search, so it was a rediscovery (author's Nov 11 update).
