    GGoogle🤖 agent launchYou use this

    Google makes Tasks in Gemini (formerly Spark) free, so Gemini can make calls, pay and book things for you

    What it means for you: Flip the Gemini app's toggle from Chat to Task and it works on a to-do for you, free in the US, Japan, Korea, Canada, Australia and India, running on the new Gemini 4 Hydrogen model. In the US it can also call businesses to compare quotes, buy with Google Pay after you approve, sign in with Google Password Manager, and take jobs you forward to gemini@google.com or text it; it stops and asks before purchases, and calling isn't available in Indiana, Louisiana, Minnesota, Montana or Nebraska.

    OAOpenAI🧠 model releaseYou use this

    OpenAI releases GPT-6.2 Astra and GPT-6.2 Sol, smarter models that use fewer words to get work done

    What it means for you: GPT-6.2 Astra is OpenAI's new top model for hard projects like documents, spreadsheets and code, and GPT-6.2 Sol is the faster everyday model that OpenAI says beats the older GPT-6 Astra on everyday life tasks at about a seventh of the cost. Both are in ChatGPT for Plus, Pro, Enterprise and Edu users and in the API; Astra's API price drops 20% to $8 in / $40 out per million tokens, while Sol keeps $2 / $10, so bots you build on them should get cheaper per job.

    Source: OpenAI · Also: OpenAI API pricing, @OpenAIDevs on X · 35.9K likes on X

    xAxAI🏢 industryYou use this

    SpaceXSI says five Grok Bots now run whole accounting jobs, from invoices to collections

    What it means for you: Each Bot owns one job (setting up accounts, billing, billing questions, matching payments, collections), learns it from a written manual over about three weeks (watch, then propose with a person approving, then work alone), and needs a person's OK for refunds and credits. SpaceXSI says invoices now go out in minutes instead of about a day; it's the company's own account, but the recipe of one clear job, written instructions and approval for money moves is a good template for any bot you set up.

🧰 Job of the day New on the site

Turn scattered booking emails into one trip sheet that flags clashes

Your flight, hotel, train and dinner confirmations are spread over five emails. Put them in one folder (or forward them) and an AI agent reads them all, writes one day-by-day trip sheet with every time, address and confirmation code, and warns you about clashes, like a flight that lands after the hotel desk closes.

✈️ Bookings & travelEasyWorks in: Hermes AgentClaude CoworkChatGPT

You end up with: One trip sheet with every booking in time order and a short list of problems to fix before you leave.

Copy today's prompt 
Turn my booking confirmations into one trip sheet and tell me what clashes. Look only at [the folder name / the files I attached]. Use only what the confirmations say; where they're silent, write "not in the confirmations".

1. List each file with a few words on what it is, and name any that aren't about my trip to [destination] on [dates].
2. Check every date's weekday and flag any that don't match.
3. Write the trip sheet day by day, in time order: each booking with its time, address and confirmation code, plus anything I must do first (online check-in, ID, deadlines).
4. Put a list of clashes at the top: overlapping bookings, places I'd reach after they close, and tight connections under [60] minutes.
5. Show me the whole sheet and wait for my yes before you save it as [trip-sheet] in that folder.

Don't book, change, cancel or email anyone.

Watch out: AI can misread a faded PDF, an odd layout or a name spelled two ways. Travel Wallet's own guide says to check each ticket's name, date, time and seat before you go.
